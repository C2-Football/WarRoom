# Owners, team names, and League life

The Wire identifies the person behind a team without discarding the team name. Current reports carry an owner-and-team byline. Career milestones, archived records, title context, and rivalry profiles lead with the owner; the associated team and season stay visible. Search accepts either name.

Identity comes from an unambiguous documented owner binding within that league, then the verified account's display name or username. A team name is never treated as proof of a person's identity. Missing owner names remain missing, and a reused roster slot cannot transfer history to a replacement manager.

## The lighter side

League life is a separate reading section. One rotating weekly feature appears in a clearly labeled front-page slot; current news retains its own headlines. Features use completed results and actual saved team names:

- Cardiac Club: close games and the people who keep finding them.
- Tough draws: a strong score meeting the wrong opponent, with median-game context when relevant.
- Bouncebacks: a loss followed by a win and a substantial scoring increase, without inventing a reason for the change.
- Team-name changes: a returning owner, a different name, and the verified seasons.
- Name Desk: short reviews of actual wording, such as alliteration, all-capital entrances, elaborate names and a friendly edit. Favorites and roasts are explicitly editorial opinions, not a vote or objective award.
- Name Archive: one verified owner's changing names across at least three distinct entries, with season labels and an editorial favorite. Reused roster slots never create this continuity.

Humor is short and about fantasy football circumstances. Facts establish the premise; a light line follows. Missing history never becomes a punchline, a fake quote, or a verdict about someone's ability. The weekly feature is stable within an edition, while the League life section exposes the other qualifying pieces. Sources and scope notes live in disclosures rather than interrupting the article.

## Draft receipts

The draft desk opens on request and reuses scored seasons already loaded by The Wire. It checks up to eight seasons first, following only the verified previous-league chain; an explicit expansion checks eight more at a time, up to 25 loaded seasons. Results arrive progressively with season-by-season coverage. Only draft metadata and completed picks are fetched, with two concurrent requests and reusable metadata caching. The desk does not download additional matchup history.

Each eligible primary snake or linear draft can supply up to four distinct articles:

- A later-pick win and a separate draft do-over compare the same position at least one round apart. Both players need at least four fantasy starts, start counts within two and at least 75% of one another, and an advantage of three points per start plus ten total points for the later pick.
- A lineup-gap story explicitly compares different workloads. More starts and a larger total are not presented as proof of a better player or owner.
- A late-round contributor comes from the second half of the draft, appears in at least half the checked weeks, and exceeds the median of at least four eligible same-position picks by two points per start.

These are scoped draft receipts, not an all-time best/worst ranking. The metric is verified starting-lineup production wherever the player was started, including after a trade. A **fantasy start** means selection in a fantasy lineup, not an NFL game played or started. Totals, start counts, averages and the checked period remain visible. Missing player scores cannot become zero; real zero and negative scores count. Bench production, retained return to the drafter, ADP grades, injury explanations and commissioner adjustments to team totals are not inferred. Keepers and auction drafts are excluded.

The selected edition requires at least four completed regular-season weeks and always retains its cutoff, even when viewing an older completed season. Linked prior seasons require every regular-season week to pass coverage checks. Draft ownership comes from the receiving roster and verified owner in that draft season, never today's draft-slot owner.

Owner notebooks collect qualifying picks across checked seasons using the same verified owner account, even when roster IDs change. Each season retains its own scoring and observation period; no combined cross-era grade is calculated. Search and the parent owner/team filter apply immediately to loaded articles, notebooks and later progress updates. Local season, owner and article-type controls narrow the receipts further. Unknown-owner roster filters remain confined to the selected season.

Account or edition changes hide old results immediately and cancel pending work. Updated loaded history rechecks an already-open desk. Failures and timeouts offer retry; a failed refresh can retain previously verified receipts with a stale notice. The coverage disclosure identifies checked, missing and ineligible seasons, so partial history never becomes an all-history claim.

The multi-league newspaper remains text-only on desktop; phones show compact identity badges. These features introduce no generated photographs or decorative graphics.
