### Rules while bidding

1. **Avoid two $65 purchases by default.** They leave $70 for five starting slots—$14 each. That can work only if you can identify credible cheap starters, not merely hope for them.
2. **A $1 starter counts just as much as a $60 starter in the weekly lineup.** Spend on all seven slots; zero bench places means no hiding an injured stash.
3. **Track the QB pool.** Eighteen mandatory starters makes waiting riskier than tonight. Buy a secure starting role before the remaining choices become uncertain jobs. Don't buy a second QB when he cannot fit your lineup.
4. **Nominate players you would actually accept at the opening price.** Don't bid simply to make someone else pay more; getting stuck is costly here.
5. **Rebudget after every win.** With $B left and N empty slots, the maximum legal bid is at most `B − (N − 1)` if the minimum is $1; verify the platform's displayed maximum. Your strategic maximum should usually be lower.
6. **Prefer separate failure risks when values are close.** Avoid concentrating too much of the lineup in one offense or one bye week. This is a tiebreak, not a reason to reject a large discount.
7. **Do not carry auction money into the waiver plan by assumption.** Treat the $200 auction and listed $10,000 FAAB as different budgets; no rollover rule was confirmed.
